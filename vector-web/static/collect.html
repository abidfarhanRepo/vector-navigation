Contribute driving data

← back to the map

Vector learns road speeds from how people actually drive. It has no real driving data yet — this page is how that changes. Everything here is opt-in, and each way of contributing asks separately.

Tier 1 · recommended

Upload a track you already recorded

works today

If you record drives with OsmAnd, Organic Maps, Strava, Komoot, Garmin or your phone's own recorder, export a .gpx and upload it here. Those apps have the background location permission a web page cannot get, so the recording is already complete and correct.

This also reaches drives you recorded months ago, which live capture can never recover. Up to a year old is accepted; older than that is refused, because a road may have been resurfaced or re-signed since and old evidence can be wrong in a way that looks right.

Nothing is uploaded until you press Check, and nothing is stored until you confirm what it found.

Tier 2

Record now, in this browser

limited by the browser

Read this before you start. A web page cannot record in the background. Both iPhone Safari and Android Chrome stop sending positions the moment the tab is hidden or the screen locks — so a drive recorded this way needs the phone awake, unlocked and on this tab for the whole journey.

  • Mount the phone and keep it charging. A screen held on for an hour is heavy on the battery.
  • If the screen locks, the recording stops. The gap is detectable and the trip is split rather than presented as a whole journey — a speed profile built from half a drive is worse than none, because it looks legitimate.
  • If you want a drive recorded without babysitting it, use Tier 1: let a recorder that has permission do the work.
Open the map to record

Recording lives on the map screen, where you can see the track being drawn. This page will grow a queue readout when live capture gets its offline queue (ticket 20).

Tier 3

Install the app

not built yet

The only way to record a drive without thinking about it is a real app with real background location. That decision is written down (ADR-0070, Android first, sideloaded) but it is not accepted yet: it is waiting on a measured battery cost for an hour of recording and a plan for how the app updates itself.

There is deliberately no download button here. Offering one before those numbers exist would be the same mistake as a record button that quietly records nothing.

What happens to what you send

Removed before it is stored

  • The first and last 200 m of every trip. Your home and your destination are not in the data. This is applied per trip, so uploading a drive in pieces does not leak its ends.
  • Fixes worse than 25 m of accuracy, and any position implying a speed no car reaches — bad fixes become wrong road speeds.
  • Positions are rounded to about a metre and times to five seconds.

Kept, briefly

  • Individual positions live in a quarantine for 72 hours and are then deleted, not archived.
  • Each trip carries a fresh random label, thrown away when the trip ends, so one trip cannot be linked to another — and there is no account, device id or IP stored beside it.

Kept for good

  • Per-stretch-of-road typical speeds, per time of week, and only once at least five separate trips cover the same stretch in the same band.
  • Precisely: that floor counts trips, not people. Because trip labels are deliberately unlinkable, five trips by five drivers and five trips by one driver are indistinguishable — so a month of your own commute can clear the floor on its own. What is published is still a fact about a road rather than about a person, but nobody should read the floor as "five people agreed to this" (ADR-0069).

Don't take our word for it: what has been discarded and what the map has learned are both live.

Where the data has come from

Three tiers land in one store through one contract, so a defect in any one of them contaminates the shared corpus. The drop rate is the signal worth watching: a tier discarding far more points is producing worse data.
